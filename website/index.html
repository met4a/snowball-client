Anti-Crash

When a mod breaks while you play, Snowball saves your world and shows what went wrong and which mod it most likely was, instead of the game closing. Press Rejoin to carry on. Running out of memory or a graphics driver crash still closes the game.

IN GAME

What you get when you press Right Shift

Real screenshots from Snowball Client 1.10.0 on Minecraft 26.2. Pick a tab.

Ten categories around one wheel

Pick a category and its modules line up beside the wheel, each with a line saying what it does. Start typing to search all of them at once.

More FPS without a manual

Pick Max FPS, Balanced or Quality and Boost sets your video options and the right optimisation mods. Turn it off and your own settings come back. Smart Culling is on from the start.

A HUD you place yourself

FPS, coordinates, compass, CPS, keystrokes, armour, potions, memory and more. The HUD editor lets you drag, resize and turn each one, with snapping, undo and presets.

PvP tools that stay fair

Your own crosshair, armour, CPS, combos, the reach of your last hit and who you are aiming at. They only report what you already see and do: nothing changes your reach or aims for you.

Every module, your way

Each module has its own keybind and settings: colours with a picker, sliders, choices, and a reset. Save the whole setup as a profile and switch between them.

Your own sky

Time Changer picks the time of day you see, and only you. The server, the mobs and everyone else keep the real time. Drag across the picture to compare.

LAUNCHER

Instances that never get in each other's way

Each instance has its own version, mods, worlds and settings, so your PvP setup never breaks your modpack. Pick one and press Play: Minecraft, Java, the loader and Snowball Client are handled for you.

Updates itself

New versions arrive in the background and install when you restart. You download an installer once.

Bring your setup

Import mods, worlds, packs, keybinds and servers from Lunar Client, Feather, Modrinth, CurseForge, Prism, MultiMC and more.

Mod browser

Search Modrinth and install in one click, at the right version for the instance, with what it needs to run.

Check mods

Known releases are recognised by fingerprint; every other jar is read on your computer and explained in plain words.

Skins page

The same skin library as in game: add, star and equip skins before you even start Minecraft.

Says what went wrong

A failed launch explains itself, with a button that fixes it where it can and the full log one click away.

Everything else the launcher does
  • Snowball Client is loaded by the launcher, never a mod file you can lose, and checked and repaired before every launch
  • Fabric API added to new Fabric instances; "this mod needs Sodium to run" with a one-click install
  • One-click mod updates, and a mod manager that catches duplicates and version conflicts
  • Java handled for you, or pick your own per instance
  • Microsoft sign-in on Microsoft's own page
  • Game files are checked once and trusted while untouched, so Play starts the game instead of re-checking thousands of files
  • A readable activity log while the game starts, crash detection and live game logs
  • Minimise it while it downloads; it tucks itself away when the game opens, and pressing Play twice never starts two games
  • Global chat with other Snowball players, and announcements
  • Your own colour, a picture behind the launcher, and a compact layout for small screens

FAQ

Questions

Is Snowball Client allowed on servers?

Snowball Client only adds visual, interface and quality-of-life features. It has no hacks such as X-ray, ESP, reach, aim assist or auto clickers. Some servers ban all client mods, so always follow each server's rules.

Does it cost anything?

No. Snowball Client is free, with nothing to buy inside it. You still need to own Minecraft: Java Edition.

Do you see my Microsoft password?

No. You sign in on Microsoft's own page. The launcher only receives a sign-in token from Microsoft, which is encrypted on your computer and never sent to us.

Who sees my skin, cape and emotes?

A skin from the skin changer is your real Minecraft skin, so everyone sees it: you at once, other players after you rejoin a server. Capes and emotes are Snowball's own, so only other Snowball players see them, on Minecraft 1.21.11 or newer. Turn off "See others' cosmetics" to see everyone's own capes instead.

Which Minecraft versions are supported?

The launcher runs any Java Edition version. Snowball Client itself is built for Minecraft 1.8.9, 1.21.11 and 26.2, and is set up automatically when you create a Fabric instance for one of them (1.8.9 uses Legacy Fabric).

Do I have to reinstall it for every update?

No. The launcher updates itself when it starts: if a new version is out it is installed and Snowball restarts into it, waiting if a game is running, and your instances, mods and settings are untouched. Two exceptions: versions 1.6.2 and older had a fault that stopped them updating, and versions 1.7.0 to 1.8.1 refuse every update with "not signed by the application owner". On any of those, download the installer above and run it once; from then on Snowball updates itself. The portable build cannot replace itself, so it tells you when a new file is on the website.

How do I move over from another launcher?

Open Instances and choose Import from another launcher. Snowball looks for Lunar Client, Feather, the Modrinth App, CurseForge, Prism, MultiMC, ATLauncher, GDLauncher and your .minecraft folder (used by the Minecraft Launcher, Badlion and LabyMod), and copies what you pick: mods, settings, resource packs, shader packs, worlds, keybinds and servers. Nothing is moved or deleted, so your old launcher keeps working exactly as it did.

Can I add my own mods, or use shaders?

Yes. Each instance has its own mods folder, and the Mods page shows what is installed, warns about duplicates and version conflicts, and lets you turn mods on and off. For shaders, install Iris and Sodium from the mod browser and your shader packs work as usual; Snowball's own features are drawn on top.

How do I know a mod is safe?

The Mods page has a Check mods button. Mods downloaded from Modrinth are recognised by their fingerprint (a SHA-1 checksum - only that is sent, never the file) and set aside as known releases. Every other jar is read on your computer and reported in plain language: whether it can run programs, read your Discord or browser folders, reach your Minecraft session, or send data to a webhook.

Will a performance profile install mods I did not ask for?

No. Profiles use the optimisation mods your instance already has and skip the rest, so picking one never downloads anything. If you would rather Snowball fetched them for you, turn on "Install optimisation mods" in Settings.

How do I open it on a Mac?

The Mac build is a beta, still on version 1.8.2, and not signed with an Apple certificate yet, so the first time macOS says it cannot check it. Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, and choose Open Anyway next to Snowball Client (or Control-click the app and choose Open). On GitHub's Mac machines it starts Minecraft 1.21.11 with Snowball Client on Apple Silicon and Intel, and 26.2 on Apple Silicon; 1.8.9 could not be checked there because those machines have no graphics card - tell us how it runs on yours.

Does Snowball Client collect anything about me?

No accounts, no tracking and no telemetry. Sign-in happens on Microsoft's own page, the token is encrypted on your computer, and logs stay on your disk with tokens redacted. Snowball's own server only hears about you for chat, your badge, capes and emotes - the privacy page lists exactly what.

Why do I not see someone's Snowball badge?

A badge only shows while that player is playing with Snowball Client. Somebody who used it once and now plays on another client has no badge, and it goes a few minutes after they close the game. You can hide your own under Menu & Accessibility, "Show others I use Snowball".

Something broke. Where do I look?

The Home page shows a readable activity log while the game starts, with the full technical log one tab away, and Settings has a button that opens the launcher's log folder. Crashes open a window with the crash report path.
